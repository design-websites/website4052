Electrical Safety

What should I do if I smell burning from an outlet? ▼

A burning smell from an outlet is a serious warning sign. Do not ignore it. You should:

  • Turn off the breaker for that circuit immediately
  • Do not touch the outlet or plug anything into it
  • Call us immediately at 425-215-5187 for emergency service
  • If you see smoke or flames, evacuate and call 911

Burning smells usually indicate electrical damage that could lead to fires if not addressed immediately.

Is it safe to have water near electrical outlets? ▼

No, water and electricity are extremely dangerous together. In bathrooms and kitchens, outlets should be:

  • GFCI protected - These outlets shut off power if they sense moisture
  • Located away from water sources - At least 6 feet from sinks, tubs, and showers

If water comes into contact with electrical equipment, turn off the breaker immediately and call us. Never touch or use electrical items that have gotten wet.

What's a GFCI outlet and why do I need them? ▼

GFCI stands for Ground Fault Circuit Interrupter. These special outlets protect you from electrical shock by instantly cutting power if they detect an electrical fault. They're required by code in:

  • Bathrooms
  • Kitchens
  • Outdoors
  • Garages
  • Any area near water

GFCI outlets can save your life. We can install them throughout your home.

How can I tell if an outlet is dangerous? ▼

Warning signs of dangerous outlets include:

  • Cracks or damage to the outlet cover
  • Loose outlet that wiggles
  • Discoloration or scorch marks
  • Warm or hot outlet (even with nothing plugged in)
  • Buzzing or humming sounds
  • Burning smell
  • Sparks when plugging things in

If you notice any of these, stop using the outlet immediately and call us for repair.

Is it safe to do electrical work myself? ▼

Electrical work can be very dangerous if not done properly. Even minor mistakes can lead to:

  • Electrical shocks or electrocution
  • Fires and property damage
  • Code violations
  • Insurance issues
  • Voided warranties

We strongly recommend hiring a licensed electrician for any electrical work. Our team can handle projects safely and in compliance with all codes.

What should I do during an electrical emergency? ▼

In an electrical emergency:

  • Call us immediately: 425-215-5187 (24/7)
  • If there's fire or smoke: Call 911 first, then call us
  • If someone is shocked: Call 911. Don't touch them if they're still in contact with electricity
  • Turn off power if safe: Use the breaker panel if you can do so safely
  • Stay away: Keep people and pets away from the problem area

What qualifies as an electrical emergency? ▼

Electrical emergencies include:

  • Complete loss of power
  • Sparking outlets or wiring
  • Burning smells from electrical equipment
  • Breakers that won't stay on
  • Electrical fire
  • Water damage near electrical systems
  • Any situation that poses immediate danger

If you're unsure, call us. We can help assess whether your situation requires emergency service.

What should I do while waiting for emergency service? ▼

While waiting for our emergency technician:

  • Turn off power if safe: Use your circuit breaker panel if you can do so safely
  • Stay away: Keep people and pets away from the problem area
  • Don't touch: Don't touch sparking wires or flooded electrical equipment
  • Gather info: Note what happened and when the problem started
  • Evacuate if needed: If there's smoke or fire, leave immediately and call 911
